# Circulation Surface

## What this node is

A circulation surface is any artifact whose job is to **move readers toward a claim** rather than to settle the claim: a headline, deck, teaser, feed item, search snippet, social card, push notification, newsletter blurb, or agent-readable summary. Under [journalism](../../journalism.md), every such surface is a *projection* from the underlying claim-provenance graph — a lossy, authority-gated emission, not an independent assertion.

The governing distinction this node draws is between transport and epistemics. A surface may intensify curiosity, compress, dramatize, or optimize for distribution; it may not invent warrant. The invariant is that no circulation surface may create a factual commitment the claim graph does not license: it cannot drop attribution, strengthen modality (turning "may" into "does" or "officials said" into "it happened"), broaden scope, erase contradiction, convert source claims into the publication's own, hide correction state, or survive unchanged after its supporting claim is corrected. Demand may create an assignment; it may not create a support edge.

## What it gathers

- [ietf-conformant-clickbait](content/ietf-conformant-clickbait/ietf-conformant-clickbait.md) — the product mission of accountable attention surfaces: clickbait that cannot lie about its own evidence, built by projecting every surface from a claim-provenance state under genre contracts with correction propagation.

## Why it matters

The circulation surface is where journalism most often launders misinformation while passing factuality checks — the body can be careful while the headline misleads. Treating surfaces as gated projections, not free text, is how attention can increase while warrant cannot be invented.

## Orientation

The invariants a surface must preserve are at the sibling [provenance-warrant](../provenance-warrant/provenance-warrant.md) node. The slogan the research closes on: make the click, preserve the warrant, propagate the correction.
