# Provenance and Warrant

## What this node is

This node holds the **invariants of claim settlement** under [journalism](../../journalism.md): the properties a claim must carry through every transformation, and the distinctions that must not be flattened. Provenance is the chain of who, what, and when produced a claim or artifact. Authenticity is that the artifact is unaltered. Credibility is that the source is trustworthy. Warrant is that the available evidence licenses publication. Truth is that the claim is so. These are five different things, and the central failure of "trust infrastructure" is to let a cryptographically signed, schema-valid, authentic artifact pass as true.

These invariants are generated directly by the [claim](../../../../../../../../contents/claim/claim.md): the base morphism brings an utterance's claim to stand *with evidence above the knowledge-bottom*, and warrant is the measure of that evidence. Settlement may revise a claim's content nonmonotonically, but its provenance must grow monotonically except for lawful, audit-marked redaction. The preserved invariants are attribution (a source's statement must not silently become the publication's unqualified assertion), modality and evidentiality (may/alleged/reported/confirmed must not strengthen), contradiction (conflicting evidence must not be smoothed into false consensus), scope and time, correction state (corrected or retracted claims must not be reused as current), and the stance boundary between reported fact and editorial interpretation.

## Why it matters

Provenance/warrant is what makes journalism *settling* rather than mere assertion: it is the structure that lets a contested claim be published as contested, a correction invalidate every dependent artifact, and an agent's tool output be recorded as an untrusted assertion until verified rather than as a fact. It is the corpus's guard against proceduralism masquerading as truth.
