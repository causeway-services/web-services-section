Rugpulls - and institutional investments - have reached new heights. Here's your Crypto News for February 17, 2025.

## Argentina's President Under Investigation for Cryptocurrency Promotion

Argentine President Javier Milei is facing a federal investigation [@reuters2025milei]after promoting a cryptocurrency named $LIBRA, which experienced a rapid collapse. The promotion, followed by the token's swift devaluation, has led to accusations of a ["rugpull" scheme](https://koios.news/2025/02/17/did-super-bowl-bettors-finance-the-libra-rugpull/), with opposition legislators threatening impeachment. The incident has also impacted Argentina's financial markets, with the S&P Merval index dropping nearly 4% on Monday. This situation underscores the potential risks when political figures engage with volatile digital assets.

## Crypto Industry Seeks Greater Political Influence Amid Regulatory Developments

The cryptocurrency industry is intensifying efforts [@apnews2025libra] to solidify its political influence and achieve mainstream acceptance, especially under the current U.S. administration. Despite mixed reactions to initiatives like the formation of a congressional working group for crypto regulation, the industry's early successes include the repeal of certain accounting rules by the SEC and an executive order considering a government reserve of cryptocurrencies. However, internal disagreements and regulatory challenges, such as those involving Tether, highlight ongoing tensions within the crypto community.

## XRP Price Declines Amid Speculation on ETF Approval

XRP's price has slipped [@barrons2025xrp] by 3% to $2.68, though it remains up 10% over the past five days. The recent price movement is driven by speculation that the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) might approve an exchange-traded fund (ETF) tracking XRP's price. The SEC's acknowledgment of an ETF application by the New York Stock Exchange on behalf of Grayscale has fueled this speculation. Approval of such an ETF could provide institutional investors with a regulated vehicle to invest in XRP, potentially boosting the token's value.

## Speculation Surrounds Bitcoin Creator's Identity

A Coinbase executive has suggested that the true identity of Bitcoin's creator [@thesun2025satoshi], known as "Satoshi Nakamoto," might be known by rival crypto exchange Kraken. This speculation arises from transactions linked to a Satoshi-controlled wallet interacting with the Canadian Bitcoin exchange CaVirtEx, later acquired by Kraken. The mystery surrounding Nakamoto's identity continues to intrigue the crypto community, with significant implications for the market, as the wallet in question holds an estimated $100 billion.

## Charles Schwab Appoints New Head of Digital Assets

Financial services giant Charles Schwab has appointed Joe Vietri [@barrons2025schwab] as the new head of digital assets, signaling the company's intent to expand its cryptocurrency offerings. Vietri, a 30-year veteran at Schwab, will develop the company's overall crypto strategy, which may include products like spot cryptocurrency trading, pending regulatory changes. This move reflects the growing interest among traditional financial institutions to integrate digital assets into their services, aiming to meet increasing customer demand and capitalize on market opportunities.

## Ethereum User Burns $1.67 Million in Unusual Transaction

In a peculiar event, an Ethereum user burned $1.67 million worth of Ether [@banklesstimes2025burn], embedding messages about brain control within the transaction. This act has sparked discussions about the motivations behind such significant financial decisions and highlights the unique ways blockchain technology can be utilized beyond traditional financial transactions.

## Person Waves Cryptocurrency Flag Atop Hollywood Sign

A person was detained after climbing [@ktla2025hollywood] the iconic Hollywood sign and waving a cryptocurrency flag. This act of protest or promotion underscores the lengths to which crypto enthusiasts will go to draw attention to digital currencies, reflecting the cultural impact and grassroots movements within the crypto community.

## Summary

Recent developments in the cryptocurrency sector highlight the complex interactions between digital assets, political figures, regulatory bodies, and traditional financial institutions. From political controversies in Argentina to major financial firms expanding into crypto, these events underscore the multifaceted nature of the evolving crypto landscape.

## What the Day's Crypto News Adds Up To

Read as separate items, this is the usual churn: a scandal, a lobbying push, an ETF rumor, a Satoshi sighting.
Read together, the day's crypto news traces a single arc—an industry trying to buy its way into legitimacy faster than its own failures can discredit it.
A sitting president is under investigation over a token he amplified.
The industry is pouring money into political influence.
A regulator's posture can move a coin's price by double digits on speculation alone.
These are not unrelated headlines.
They are the same system, viewed from four windows: one where crypto reaches for state power, one where it buys access to it, one where it waits on its permission, and one where it retreats into myth.
The throughline worth holding onto is how much of this market now runs on proximity to politics rather than on anything the technology does.
When that is true, the regulatory weather is not background to the story.
It is the story.

It is worth sitting with how strange that is.
A technology that was pitched as an exit from political institutions now spends most of its energy lobbying, litigating, and waiting on them.
The Satoshi rumor that resurfaces on slow days is almost nostalgic by comparison—a reminder of an origin myth in which the whole point was that no person, and no state, was in charge.
The day's crypto news is the distance between that myth and the present, measured in scandals and lobbying budgets.
