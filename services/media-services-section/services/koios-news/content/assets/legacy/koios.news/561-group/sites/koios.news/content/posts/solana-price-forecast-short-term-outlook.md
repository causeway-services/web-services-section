Solana ($SOL) has been on a rollercoaster ride lately, and investors are watching closely to see where it goes next. With big token unlocks coming, FUD around "PvP meme casino" scams, and a possible ETF in the works, there’s a lot at play. Let’s break down how these factors could impact SOL’s price over different timeframes.

## Short-Term: Selling Pressure from FTX Unlocks

One of the biggest short-term concerns for SOL is the upcoming unlock of tokens from the FTX estate. Around **12 million SOL** will be released in March and April, with another **9.75 million SOL** scheduled for later this year. When a large number of tokens hit the market, it usually increases selling pressure, which can drive prices down.

Adding to this, the last batch of FTX's **14 million unlocked SOL** was sold at **$20-$30 per token**. Some investors fear that new unlocks could lead to more discounted sales, dragging SOL lower.

Analysts have identified key **bearish price targets at $160 and $130**—levels where SOL could find support if sell pressure increases.

## Mid-Term: Can the ETF Narrative Bring Buyers?

On the flip side, a major potential catalyst is the talk of a **Solana ETF**. Big investment firms like **VanEck, 21Shares, and Galaxy Digital** have applied for a Solana ETF, and if approved, it could bring fresh demand.

Right now, **Ethereum ETF approvals are expected in May**, and if that goes well, a Solana ETF could gain momentum. This could help offset the selling pressure from the FTX unlocks and keep prices from dropping too much.

## Long-Term: Strong Community & Adoption Keep SOL Relevant

Despite short-term volatility, **Solana’s fundamentals remain strong**. The network has been seeing high activity, with **millions of new addresses being created daily**. Its ecosystem, known for **fast transactions and low fees**, continues to attract projects, developers, and users.

Even with occasional **network outages**, the Solana Foundation is actively working on improvements, which reassures long-term holders. Many analysts believe that **SOL could reclaim $200+** once short-term pressures ease, and some even predict a move toward **$280 if market conditions improve**.

## Final Thoughts: Buy the Dip or Wait?

SOL is currently trading **around $183**, down from recent highs. If prices drop below $160, it could present a **buy-the-dip opportunity** for long-term investors. However, short-term traders should be cautious, as the **FTX token unlocks could lead to more volatility**.

Overall, **the next few months will be key**. If Solana weathers the selling pressure and gets closer to an ETF approval, its long-term outlook remains bullish. But in the short term, expect some turbulence.

## What Solana's Supply Cliff Says About Crypto's Hidden Clocks

A price forecast is the least interesting thing about this situation, and the easiest to get wrong, so I will not pretend to one.
What is worth naming is the structure underneath it.
Solana is being pulled by two clocks running at different speeds.
The first is a supply clock: tokens from the FTX estate and other unlocks arriving on a schedule that has nothing to do with how the network is performing, and everything to do with bankruptcy timelines and vesting contracts signed years ago.
The second is a narrative clock: the slow, uneven build of an ETF story, institutional curiosity, and a genuinely active developer and user base.

The honest forecast is that these clocks do not synchronize.
Supply cliffs are precise—you can mark the date on a calendar.
Narrative is diffuse, and arrives when it arrives.
That mismatch is most of what "short-term pressure, long-term strength" actually means once you strip the optimism out of it: known sellers on a fixed timetable, against uncertain buyers on no timetable at all.
In a market with abundant liquidity, the buyers show up and the cliffs get absorbed.
In a thinner market, the cliffs land harder, because there is less standing capital to catch them.
Solana may well be fine, or better than fine.
But the thing to watch is not a price target.
It is whether the patient, real adoption can keep arriving faster than the mechanical supply that is already scheduled to hit.

That framing also explains why "buy the dip or wait" is the wrong question, or at least an incomplete one.
A dip caused by a scheduled unlock is a different animal from a dip caused by a collapse in confidence, even when the chart looks identical.
The first is the market digesting a known quantity of supply; the second is the market revising its story about what Solana is worth at all.
Telling them apart in the moment is hard, and getting it wrong is how people confuse a discount for a warning.
The useful discipline is not predicting the price.
It is knowing which clock you are actually trading against.
