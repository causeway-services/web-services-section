## Introduction to the Challenge
At the southern edge of Traverse City, Michigan, a program is underway to support the next generation of farmers, as the U.S. faces an aging farmer population and the impacts of climate breakdown, which can lead to crop failure food prices, with the Great Lakes Incubator Farm attracting students from all over the country to learn essential skills like pest management, tractor operation, and farm business planning, as reported in [Becoming a farmer is hard.
This Michigan program wants to help](https://grist.org/food-and-agriculture/becoming-a-farmer-is-hard-this-michigan-program-wants-to-help/).
Rachel Greenberg, a 33-year-old student farmer from Indianapolis, highlights the challenges of getting into farming, stating that "nobody gets into farming for sane reasons, other than the sanity of knowing where your food comes from and just general health."
The program's focus on hands-on learning and support is crucial, given the significant barriers to entry for new farmers, including high upfront costs, limited access to land, and the shifting climate.
